How it works

In clinical supply the same question comes up every week: how many kits should go to each site, and when? Send too few and a patient shows up with no kit. Send too many and they sit on a shelf until they expire.

I built this to compare a few reorder rules (the logic that decides when a site gets a shipment and how big it is). None of it is real trial data. I made the trials up so every rule could be tested on exactly the same ones.

How a run works

  1. Make up a trial. 78 sites, 10 countries, 500 patients, two arms. A patient gets a kit at enrollment and then one every 4 weeks. I put the messy parts in too: sites open late, start slow, some stall, visits slip a week either way, and now and then one gets missed.
  2. Play it week by week under each rule. Same patients, same visits. Only the shipping changes.
  3. Do it again, 200 times, with a different made-up trial each time. That's the Monte Carlo part. One trial can be lucky. 200 can't all be.
  4. Count what happened: kits short, kits expired, kits sitting on shelves, shipments sent.

About the machine learning

The last rule uses LightGBM, a tree-based model that's common in demand forecasting. I trained it on 40 separate made-up trials to predict what each site will need over the next 1 to 8 weeks.

I expected it to win. It didn't. It lands about where Expected visits does, a little worse (roughly 1 more kit short per trial). Looking back, that makes sense: most kits go to patients already on treatment, and their visits are already on the calendar. There isn't much left for a model to guess.

The dial and switches

Each position was run ahead of time (200 trials each), which is why the page doesn't need a server. I didn't re-tune the rules for each setting, on purpose. I wanted to see how they hold up when things don't go to plan.

  • Safety cushion: how many extra kits a rule keeps around. Less means leaner shelves and more stockouts.
  • Shipping time +1 week: every shipment takes a week longer.
  • Shelf life: kits last 12, 24 (normal) or 36 months.
  • Enrollment speed: sites enroll 30% slower or faster than planned.
  • Enrollment shock: a surge (30% of sites suddenly enroll 3 times faster for 12 weeks) or a stall (30% of sites stop enrolling for 16 weeks).

What it doesn't do

  • It's all made up, so use it to compare rules, not to forecast a real study.
  • One kit type per arm. One depot, and it never runs out. Shipping times are fixed by region.
  • No costs. Whether fewer kits beats fewer shipments depends on the drug and the couriers.
